Case Study

Building FIB UI Anti Kekerasan Seksual from Zero

A people and organization case study in organization design, recruitment, onboarding, learning and development, stakeholder management, governance, and accountability.

Overview

In early 2025, I founded FIB UI Anti Kekerasan Seksual, a student-led organization at the Faculty of Humanities, Universitas Indonesia, focused on sexual violence prevention, education, advocacy, and student support.

The organization was created in response to the need for a dedicated faculty-level student body that could address sexual violence issues in a consistent, structured, and responsible way. Because the issue is highly sensitive, the challenge was not only gathering people, but building a credible organization with the right structure, members, governance, and shared ethical standards.

The HR Challenge

Because the organization worked on a sensitive issue, members could not be selected only based on enthusiasm. They needed to be reliable, empathetic, discreet, accountable, and able to understand confidentiality and survivor-centered values.

How could I build a structured, credible, and psychologically safe organization from zero while ensuring that the right people were selected, trained, engaged, and supported by a clear governance system?

My Role

As founder and initiator, I led the early development of the organization from concept to execution. My responsibilities included designing the organization structure, developing the big design, coordinating endorsement campaigns, planning recruitment and interviews, supporting onboarding and culture building, supporting member learning programs, coordinating with stakeholders, and contributing to governance documents such as AD/ART, RKAT, and evaluation reports.

The organization was designed with 5 functional divisions covering education and campaign, advocacy or support-related work, creative communication, external relations, and internal operations.

  • Developed AD/ART as the constitutional basis of the organization.
  • Established RKAT on 7 March 2025 as the official work plan, budget, and accountability document.
  • Defined formal roles across chairperson, vice chairperson, secretary, treasurer, coordinators, executive board, and staff.
  • Created meeting, consultation, decision-making, regeneration, and cadre development mechanisms.

The recruitment process followed the flow: awareness → application → screening → interview → role placement → onboarding. Open recruitment ran from 28 February to 3 March 2025, followed by interviews and selection on 4–6 March 2025.

  • Candidate assessment covered mission alignment, reliability, commitment, collaboration, sensitivity toward sexual violence issues, ethical awareness, confidentiality, role fit, and divisional needs.
  • The process resulted in 40+ members placed across 5 divisions.

The internal culture was built around four main values: confidentiality, empathy, accountability, and collaboration. These values guided how members communicated, worked across divisions, handled sensitive information, and represented the organization externally.

Member capacity building was designed so members had shared knowledge, ethical awareness, and practical understanding before representing the organization externally.

The organization built credibility through coordination with student governance bodies, BEM FIB UI, LFK FIB, FISIP Anti KS, Komite/Satgas PPKS UI, Yayasan Pulih, SAFEnet, external experts, and the broader FIB UI student community.

Public communication channels included Instagram, X, Linktree, email, and OA Line to introduce the mission, share educational resources, and make support or reporting-related information more accessible.

The organization developed evaluation mechanisms such as LPJTT to document achievements, challenges, training attendance, pre-test and post-test results, scheduling challenges, external partner coordination, Terms of Reference preparation, RKAT revision, member data management, documentation, and meeting archive improvement.

Key Learning

This experience shaped how I understand people strategy. HR is not only recruitment or administration; it is designing systems where people can contribute with clarity, safety, and purpose. A strong organization needs the right structure, the right people, the right culture, and the right learning system to turn an idea into sustainable impact.
